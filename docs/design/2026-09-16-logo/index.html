Sirdar logo marks, six directions

A sirdar is the head guide of a Himalayan expedition, the person who leads the climb. The app ships the Wails default icon today; these are six original marks on a mountain motif in the colours of Nepal's flag, each drawn on a 512 grid for the macOS squircle and checked at 16.

16 September 2026Vector only, no third-party marks, no fonts inside the marksWordmark set in Newsreader and Figtree
Crimson #DC143C Blue #003893 White #FFFFFF Paper #FAF7EC Ink #17181C

What each variation shows. The mark at 512 on a light (paper) and a dark (ink) tile, the 128, 32 and 16 steps, a true 16-pixel raster enlarged without smoothing, the 16px glyph in a menu bar, the lockup with the wordmark, one line of rationale and the main tradeoff.

How to read the tiles. The squircle is the icon; macOS draws no frame around it. Blue is swapped for paper on the dark tile because #003893 on #17181C is 1.6:1 and vanishes; crimson stays on both. The dark tile is what the icon becomes under a dark-tinted Dock.

Rationale

Two overlapping peaks, blue behind and crimson in front, with a single route drawn up the higher one and a marker where the route ends just below the summit: the sirdar's job is the line, not the mountain.

Main tradeoff

The route is a 22-unit stroke, so at 16px it disappears and the icon becomes two triangles; the two-colour mountain still reads, but the idea does not.

With the wordmark

Sirdar
Sirdar

Rationale

Nepal's flag is the only non-rectangular national flag; one crimson pennon with the blue fimbriation carries a white summit where the flag carries its sun, so the mark is a country and a mountain in one shape.

Main tradeoff

It reads as a flag first and a mountain second, and the weight sits in the lower-left corner, so the lockup needs the wordmark on the right to balance.

With the wordmark

Sirdar
Sirdar

Rationale

One continuous rope stroke rises to the summit and descends through a loop, the knot a guide ties for the people behind; the blue summit dot is the only second colour.

Main tradeoff

A 44-unit line is 1.4px at 16px and the loop closes into a blob; it is the most distinctive silhouette at 128 and above and the weakest below 32.

With the wordmark

Sirdar
Sirdar

Rationale

A single crimson peak with the initial cut out of it as a switchback trail: the top bowl of the S is narrow because the mountain is narrow there, so the letter and the terrain agree.

Main tradeoff

The cut is a true hole, so it works on any ground, but at 16px the S is a 1.25px scratch and the icon is a red triangle; the letter only appears from 32 up.

With the wordmark

Sirdar

Rationale

A compass whose north needle is the peak and whose pivot is a summit marker; it says navigation and direction rather than scenery, which is closer to what a sirdar does.

Main tradeoff

Compass roses are a crowded category (Safari, maps, travel apps), and the mountain reading depends on the crimson triangle being seen as a peak rather than a needle.

Rationale

Two shapes only: a crimson peak and a disc rising behind its shoulder, the flag's sun moved to the sky; nothing in it can fail at 16px.

Main tradeoff

It is the most generic of the six (triangle-and-circle is a common outdoor-brand construction) and depends on the colour pair to be Sirdar's rather than anyone's.
